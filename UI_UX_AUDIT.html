OpenKhmer UI/UX audit

October 8, 2026 · Audit completed before implementation

Purpose: introduce independent Khmer OCR research and invite sponsors, technical collaborators and startup programs. This is one landing page, not an OCR application.

Direction retained: paper-colored background, dark typography, restrained lime accent, original Khmer document illustration, factual research milestones and a straightforward email contact path.

Scope and evidence: all seven sections, header and footer reviewed visually at 1280px desktop, 768px tablet and 390px mobile; source and DOM measurements checked. No P0 broken primary journey found. There are no inputs, forms, search, tables, modals, sidebar, avatars, or account flows to audit. Loading, error and success states for nonexistent backend operations are not applicable.

Screen-by-screen findings

  • Header: alignment and common container work well. Add current-section feedback and reliable mobile disclosure behavior.
  • Hero: the title, research stage and original artwork are a clear hierarchy. Keep the illustration and typography direction. Raise the stage label to 14px and make the email action explicit.
  • Mission: the two-column layout is coherent. Shorten the generic prose and reduce the 100px boundaries. The intended-outcome row should use plain labels rather than arrows suggesting links.
  • Research: three priorities are easy to compare. Preserve their distinct illustrations. Make body copy 16px and stop shrinking it on tablet. Illustration captions are decorative secondary content.
  • Concept preview: the illustration remains honest and useful. The input/output labels, tiny tabs and repeated explanatory text need the most work. Preserve working keyboard tab controls; fix script metadata.
  • Progress: the counts are credible and clearly separated from model accuracy. Improve milestone stage legibility and match section rhythm to the rest of the page.
  • Sponsorship: the lime background correctly emphasizes the primary goal. Replace the unrelated button style, normalize the list typography, and retain the no-affiliation statement.
  • FAQ: native details/summary is appropriate and keyboard capable. Keep it; normalize answer text to 16px.
  • Footer: aligned branding is sound. Small link hit areas and 9–12px metadata need clearer sizing and spacing.

A. Top 10 problems hurting the design most

The numbered issue register above is the ranked top ten. The most material are small essential text, footer targets and Khmer language metadata. Section spacing, shared actions and mobile recovery follow. Responsive CSS maintenance is lowest priority.

B. Quick wins

  1. Set provenance, stage and tab text to 14px.
  2. Set link/control targets to 44–48px.
  3. Correct Khmer language tagging.
  4. Reduce section boundaries to 64px, 48px mobile.
  5. Make email actions and navigation labels consistent.

C. Design system changes needed

Spacing: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64px. Use 24px component gutters, 32px heading separation, 64px desktop sections and 48px mobile sections. Artwork coordinates are deliberately exempt.

Typography: hero retains responsive display type; section headings 36–48px; card headings 20–24px; body 16px/1.65; controls and important status 14px/1.5; secondary metadata 12px/1.5. Use existing Arial and self-hosted Khmer font.

Colors: retain paper #f8f9f3, ink #20241e, muted #65695f, line #dce0d5 and lime #c5f368. Add named surface, accent-text, hover and focus tokens. The standard muted text is sufficiently contrasted; no blanket contrast failure is claimed.

Shape/elevation: keep flat rectangular controls and one-pixel borders. Reserve offset shadows and rotations for artwork. Do not introduce gradients, glass effects, new decorations or motion.

D. Components to standardize

  • ActionLink: primary email action and text link variants.
  • Navigation: common labels, active section, mobile disclosure and keyboard recovery.
  • Preview tabs: shared readable label and target metrics.
  • Section heading/kicker and narrative/card typography: common CSS tokens.
  • Milestone statuses, metadata, border and surfaces: shared CSS roles.

No reason to add components for forms, tables or modals that do not exist.

E. Pages needing most work

There is one route. Highest effort belongs to its concept preview and shared navigation/footer; next is the narrative spacing and sponsorship action. The hero requires a targeted polish pass, not a redesign.

F. Good decisions to preserve

  • Original Khmer document art and calm paper/lime identity.
  • Consistent main container alignment.
  • Clear research-stage positioning and source-backed corpus counts.
  • No fabricated partners, accuracy claims or live OCR.
  • Native FAQ behavior and working tab keyboard controls.
  • Visible focus and reduced-motion support.
  • Direct contact with no unnecessary lead-capture form.

G. Recommended implementation order

  1. Foundations: type roles, spacing tokens, surfaces and section rhythm.
  2. Shared components: one ActionLink and consistent tab/status metrics.
  3. Navigation: stable disclosure, Escape, focus recovery and current-section feedback.
  4. Individual sections: concise mission/concept copy and correct language annotations.
  5. States and responsive polish: hover/pressed/focus, comfortable targets and narrow wrapping.
  6. Final consistency: consolidate styles, check desktop/tablet/mobile, build, lint and type-check.

Verification limits

This is a visual, source and keyboard audit, not a formal WCAG certification or user study. Real screen-reader pronunciation and sponsor conversion have not been measured. Email links are checked without sending a message. Previously documented development-tool dependency advisories remain separate from this design audit.
